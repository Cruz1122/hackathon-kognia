# Stateful omnichannel backend

The backend keeps operational memory in `AgentSnapshot` per tenant-scoped conversation. `AgentState` is a versioned application contract; chat messages remain the transcript, while facts carry provenance, key actions describe meaningful changes, and tool history records bounded results. `GET /conversations/{id}/agent-state` is an authenticated tenant-scoped view. The authenticated `PUT /conversations/{id}/whatsapp` endpoint requires an operator assertion of verified identity and an existing matching customer phone before it links the WhatsApp identity. `POST /conversations/{id}/agent-resume` clears a human handoff and pending consent after operator action.

Sentiment evaluators `satisfaction` and `frustration` use a 5-level ordinal scale (`very_low`, `low`, `neutral`, `high`, `very_high`) plus `unknown`, so a UI can place them on a 5-level ruler. Decision-critical signals (`confirmation`, `callback_request`, `integrity`, `human`) and `intent` keep their discrete labels because the runtime policy and safety checks depend on their exact values.

Sentiment labels remain internal telemetry and are translated into dynamic service behavior on each turn rather than exposed as customer-facing emotion labels. High friction reduces verbosity and limits questions to one essential question; very high friction prioritizes a single actionable step over small talk. Low satisfaction focuses the response on unresolved requests and evidenced failures; very low satisfaction suggests a concrete alternative to a failed approach. Low friction permits a natural pace and useful explanations; high satisfaction permits a cordial flow without unnecessary reconfirmations. High friction takes precedence over a positive tone. Missing/neutral signals add no special behavior. The model-selected conversation path follows the same friction rule while deciding its own wording and tool selection. Voice and WhatsApp share this guidance, prohibit attributing emotions to the customer, and apologize only for evidenced service issues. Sentiment never authorizes tools, bypasses required consent, or substitutes for requested human handoff.

The conversation runtime loads up to 24 persisted messages before Jev observation, including messages from other channels. Jev sees the conversation ID, recent messages, facts, pending action and tool evidence; partial classifications preserve valid answers. The LLM selects tools even when Jev is unavailable. A booking write requires a presented, unchanged, unexpired proposal and a semantic `confirmation=explicit` verdict. A separate, high-confidence `callback_request=explicit` signal can authorize only an immediate call to the verified current phone. Unavailable or invalid verdicts do not authorize, and broad intent labels never authorize effects. Confirmed pending tools execute before response generation. Free-form drafts are buffered; unsupported integrity verdicts trigger a tool-free rewrite with conversation and domain context preserved. Missing integrity verdicts do not prove correctness. Voice proposals become presentable only after playback acknowledgement; WhatsApp proposals after delivered/read. Human handoff pauses automation until authenticated operator resume.

The Telnyx generator is consumed entirely by one producer task so operational `ContextVar` tokens survive all yields and are reset in the owning task. A bounded queue separates event consumption and holding speech. Planning text from tool-call rounds is discarded rather than concatenated to the final answer. Once response text is emitted, cleanup failures never append a second fallback. Presence checks wait for outstanding playback marks as well as agent work to finish before starting their 10-second idle window.

## Conversational benchmark

`backend/scripts/benchmark_agent.py` runs 10 Spanish conversations with real configured LLM/Jev and PostgreSQL snapshots. It supports a default STT-error suite and a separate typing-error suite. It refuses database names without `benchmark`; use a dedicated migrated database, not customer data. PSTN dialing is mocked and no Meta messages are sent. Each conversation has fresh identity/state and playback acknowledgements are simulated. Both suites cover booking, corrections, cancellation, callback, group-size limits and human handoff. The typing suite specifically includes `ola soi`, `kiero resurva`, `sii porfa asla`, `dale confimalo`, `no olbidalo` and `sii yamame`; those are not representative acoustic STT errors.

The simulated customer can answer an actual pending proposal up to two extra times; the report records `extra_confirmation_turns` so avoidable reconfirmation is not hidden. Objective checks use persisted effect results and exact booking arguments. Naturalness checks are limited lexical regression gates, not a complete quality or safety score; manually inspect transcripts for unsupported promises and unnecessary questions. The report records provider/model, turn latency, proposals, tool results and final state. Text injection does not measure Sherpa audio accuracy or real Telnyx/Piper playback.

The default `--suite stt` uses the separate fixtures in `scripts/agent_stt_cases.py`: phonetic word substitutions, deleted required details, early endpoints/continuation segments, number substitutions and truncated conditions. These are hand-authored plausible ASR hypotheses, not a measured or calibrated Sherpa error distribution. Each turn reports `clean_utterance` (hidden oracle), `transcript` (the only current input sent to the agent), `error_type` and `recoverability`. Original meaning is never injected into the conversation history. Clarification turns are part of the client simulation, not secret repairs to agent input. The original misspelling suite remains available explicitly as `--suite typing`.

Report recoverable/clarifiable cases separately from an irreversible information-loss control: deleting a negation can turn "No confirmes todavía" into a complete affirmative command. An effect in that control violates the original utterance even when it follows the supplied transcript. A text-only agent cannot reliably infer the missing negation; do not misreport this as recoverable interpretation success or silently correct it with the oracle.

Goal completion after scripted clarifications is not immediate semantic recovery. Review the response to each corrupt turn separately: did it preserve known facts, ask for a missing detail, accept an undetectable substituted number, or misinterpret the task? Reports expose `model_generated_turns` and `policy_turns`; human-handoff policy replies are not LLM-generation evidence. `real_provider` is true only when every turn was generated by a model provider, including in human-handoff scenarios. Lexical naturalness gates do not prove correct interpretation or authorize safety claims.

Run from `backend/` after applying migrations to a dedicated DB:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/benchmark_agent.py --database-url "$BENCHMARK_DATABASE_URL" --output /tmp/opencode/agent-benchmark.json
```

The demo/test chain now uses `gpt-4.1` after comparison against `gpt-4o-mini` and `gpt-4.1-mini`; the production chain is unchanged. `OPENAI_MODEL` overrides the OpenAI model explicitly for comparison or rollback. This choice has a higher token cost than the previous mini default. Keep full benchmark reports outside customer-data directories.

`AgentOperation` durably records write intent before effects. Restaurant availability is simulated; the demonstration booking result is deterministic and lives in this ledger rather than a live restaurant backend. Replays return the recorded result. A started/uncertain external operation is not repeated blindly. For Telnyx callbacks, the idempotent command identifier and base64 server-owned `client_state` correlate signed provider events with the original conversation; the first verified event reconciles a lost dial response. Events whose signed correlation cannot be mapped are ignored. Existing Telnyx event deduplication is process-local, while business write intents are PostgreSQL-backed.

## WhatsApp

`GET/POST /webhooks/whatsapp` implement Meta challenge verification, raw-body HMAC-SHA256 validation, a 1 MiB webhook envelope limit, event-batch parsing, durable deduplication, and ACK only after database commit. PostgreSQL inbox/outbox rows are authoritative; Redis is only a wake-up. The worker also scans pending/stale rows to recover from lost queue pushes or worker restarts. Each event is serialized with a PostgreSQL advisory lock. Outgoing uncertain sends are not retried automatically because provider acceptance may have occurred despite a client timeout. Delivery statuses are monotonic and are stored separately from user messages.

Text, button/list replies and incoming audio voice notes are normalized into the same conversation. Audio metadata/download uses the authenticated Meta Graph endpoint, HTTPS media-host allowlisting, a 16 MiB bound, SHA-256 verification and the existing Sherpa `ffmpeg` decoder with a 120-second bound. The transcript is capped at 8,000 characters. Other message types receive a safe textual failure; outbound audio and general image/document understanding are not supported.

WhatsApp text is sent only within the last-user-message 24-hour service window. Outside that window an explicit opt-in and an approved template are required; no arbitrary user text is injected into the template. Voice calls do not reset that window. Operator binding is intended for a demo number and requires an already stored customer phone. The endpoint trusts the authenticated operator's `identity_verified` assertion; it does not itself perform OTP verification.

When a bound call ends, the backend enqueues one continuation outbox event (deduplicated by call id) and wakes the worker. Within the service window, the worker generates a bounded apology and transcript-grounded recap, with Jev tone guidance and a safe fallback. Outside the window, opt-in and an approved template remain mandatory. Only an accepted continuation records the system event "Llamada transferida a WhatsApp"; generation alone does not claim a transfer or sent message.

## Continuous call monitor

Every inbound PSTN call creates a new conversation even when its phone matches an existing customer or WhatsApp binding; any stale session attachment/history is discarded on successful persistence. The phone may identify the customer, but never selects conversation history. Only agent-initiated outbound callbacks resume an existing conversation, using the signed, server-owned callback operation in `restore_outbound`.

At hangup, continuation can reuse an existing operator-verified WhatsApp destination for the same customer, tenant and configured sender. The identity's current routing moves to the fresh conversation; no previous transcript, snapshot or facts are copied. Consent, template configuration and the real WhatsApp inbound timestamp stay unchanged: voice never opens the service window. No binding is created from caller ID. Without an existing verified destination, continuation is not sent.

Channel event payloads pin a server-owned `_conversation_id`, distinct from the identity's current routing. Before routing moves, legacy events without a pin are assigned their original conversation. New inbound events and replies are pinned on creation; workers lock the identity, refresh the event and validate its pinned tenant/conversation before processing. Queued messages, replies, recaps and delivery acknowledgements keep their original context while future WhatsApp messages use the new route. Repeated hangups with an existing continuation event do not move routing backwards. Continuation is queued before enrichment so an enrichment enqueue error cannot suppress post-call messaging.

Routing transitions retain `_route_previous_conversation_id` and `_route_changed_at` on the continuation event. A delayed inbound webhook uses its Meta timestamp against this transition history rather than its arrival time to choose the conversation. Meta timestamps have one-second resolution; an inbound whose second straddles a transition is retained as `routing_ambiguous` and is not processed automatically. Those retained messages require operator review; no content-based routing guess is made. Delivery acknowledgements instead correlate the original outbound event ID and pinned context.

Fresh call onboarding in both the browser demo and Telnyx introduces the assistant and asks for the customer's name before collecting booking details. Telnyx onboarding starts at the media `start` event, even if the caller speaks first; STT keeps receiving speech during the greeting and barge-in preserves up to two seconds of PCM. The browser demo sends and persists the initial welcome on a new conversation and can cancel its TTS on barge-in. Presence prompts cannot precede onboarding. Outbound reconnection resumes its signed conversation instead of repeating onboarding. These scheduling guarantees do not prove an acoustic end-to-end latency bound.

## Model-selected tools

Every stateful customer turn is sent through the normal model tool-calling path. The model interprets conversational context, asks for missing details naturally, and chooses from the registered tools; there is no parallel reservation intent/slot planner or fixed question sequence. Consecutive user fragments remain in the conversation history. Tool schemas and handlers remain authoritative for argument validation and actual outcomes.

Tool selection does not grant permission to perform a write. `ToolRegistry` still validates every call, and write tools require a persisted proposal and explicit confirmation of the exact presented arguments. Changes or cancellations withdraw that proposal; successful and uncertain operations retain their existing idempotency/reconciliation behavior. A separate high-confidence, current-turn callback-request verdict may authorize only `call_customer` to the verified destination, with no user-controlled phone argument. Handoff blocks further automated actions. Responses about completed actions are grounded in the tool result, and pending booking proposals are presented with their exact terms before confirmation.

After `create_booking` succeeds, the voice response includes a brief farewell and the call ends only after playback: the browser demo waits for its PCM queue to drain, while Telnyx waits for the final media mark before issuing the Call Control hangup command. A proposal, failed write or uncertain result does not trigger call termination.

Jev provides per-turn sentiment, human-handoff, intent and confirmation observations; it does not choose reservation tools or infer reservation fields. The conversational model chooses tools from the registry, while policy and tool execution remain the safety boundary. Callback destination lookup remains tenant-scoped and independent of the WhatsApp identity's current route; signed outbound restoration keeps the operation's conversation ID and verified destination, never a phone argument.

`--suite guided` benchmarks the reported voice sequence, garbled fragments after known details, AM/PM completion, condition changes, cancellation, group limits, human requests and callback from WhatsApp. It reports `one_question_gate` and `interpreted_turns`. Calls remain mocked; completing a text benchmark does not validate real PSTN ringing or actual caller barge-in acoustics.

Compose copies backend code into the image rather than mounting the source. Local code edits and `scripts/build.sh` do not update the running API/worker. Apply backend changes with `docker compose build backend` followed by `docker compose up -d --no-deps backend worker` when no calls are active. Recreating the API interrupts live media and in-process monitors; PostgreSQL and recording volumes are retained. Verify the running image/code as well as health before testing a new call.

Calls and recording snapshots are individual voice legs attached to a conversation, not conversation termination. `/ws/calls/monitor` retains `subscribe.call` compatibility, follows the attached conversation, polls up to 100 recent WhatsApp/system messages once per second (including separate-worker writes), and switches to newer live voice legs in the same tenant/conversation. `call.snapshot` identifies the current leg; sequence deduplication must use `(call_id, seq)`, while durable messages expose `message_id`. WhatsApp messages use `transcript.final` with `payload.channel=whatsapp`; system messages use `conversation.event`. The frontend stays mounted after ENDED and reconnects audio when the next leg appears. Recordings remain per-leg snapshots; there is no continuous audio while the phone is disconnected. Opening an already saved call still shows its replay.

Callbacks retain the conversation history and resume the pending subject instead of requesting the name again. Each newly confirmed callback proposal has a distinct operation ID; retries within the same turn reuse its result. Idle voice receives a varied presence check after more than 10 seconds, suppressed during speech or agent work. Telnyx slow turns may emit one holding phrase from a pool, followed by the real answer; failures use a distinct recovery pool rather than an unfulfilled promise to wait. WhatsApp customer bubbles use a user-requested green exception to the gooey palette.

## Minimal configuration

Existing `DATABASE_URL`, `REDIS_URL`, LLM and Telnyx settings are reused. New secrets, only in `backend/.env` (not source control):

```dotenv
TYPESAFE_API_KEY=
WHATSAPP_ACCESS_TOKEN=
WHATSAPP_APP_SECRET=
WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN=
WHATSAPP_PHONE_NUMBER_ID=
```

The Graph API version (`v23.0`) and Jev model (`jev-1.13.0`) are deliberately pinned in code. The Meta business account must subscribe to the `messages` webhook field, grant messaging permissions, and have approved templates. Telnyx must have an outbound-enabled Voice API application and verified origin number. `TELNYX_PHONE_NUMBER`, `TELNYX_CONNECTION_ID`, and existing webhook/signature settings supply those values. WhatsApp callbacks/notifications are not a replacement for a genuine human operator queue; human handoff is recorded and automation is paused.

## Migration, operation, and rollback

Apply Alembic revision `0007_stateful_agent` after taking the project's normal database backup. It is additive and does not backfill or rewrite existing conversation messages. Revert by stopping the new app/worker paths and deploying the previous app; retain new tables while diagnosing. Do **not** downgrade while snapshots, bindings or operation history must be retained. External reservations/calls/messages cannot be rolled back by a database downgrade.

Run `python -m app.worker` alongside the API. Pending WhatsApp rows are recovered from PostgreSQL; stale externally-sending rows become `uncertain` and need provider/operator reconciliation, never an automatic resend. Keep webhook secrets out of logs and never log message/media bodies. Configure access controls and retention in Meta/TypeSafe accounts before sending customer data.

## Verification boundaries

Unit tests cover exact confirmation binding, signature bytes, batch parsing, window/template rules and media validation. The disposable PostgreSQL integration suite exercises migration, cross-tenant isolation, persistence/replay, turn serialization, integrity gating, inbox/outbox, callback and cancellation. Build and HTTP health smoke do not prove real Meta, Jev or Telnyx account configuration; a sandbox message, voice note, delivered callback and outbound test call remain required before production use. `ffmpeg` already exists in the backend container image.
